Field guide

Build or renovate a pitcher's mound

How to repair and rebuild the high-stress areas of the mound and home plate, with a coverage chart showing how many clay bricks and bags you need.

Download the PDFGet a quote

The front of the pitching rubber, the landing area, and the batter's and catcher's boxes take more punishment than the rest of the field combined. Use GameTime Mound Clay and GameTime Clay Bricks to construct, rebuild or repair them.

1 bag = 4 sq ftOne 50 lb bag of GameTime Mound Clay covers 4 square feet at a depth of 1 inch.

Repair or rebuild?

Repairing worn spots

  1. Broom off all loose dirt from the stress area and lightly water it.
  2. Add GameTime Mound Clay in 1 inch layers.
  3. Tamp the clay to a firm, level surface.
  4. Repeat steps 2 and 3 until the new surface is level with the old surface.

Rebuilding the area

  1. Remove 2 to 4 inches of existing soil from the stress area and lightly water it.
  2. Add GameTime Mound Clay in 1 inch layers.
  3. Tamp the clay to a firm, level surface, adding water as necessary.
  4. Repeat steps 2 and 3 until the new surface is level with the old surface.

Rebuilding with clay bricks: 6 steps

  1. Measure. Measure out the pitcher's mound using the recommended dimensions.
  2. Cut out. Cut out and remove all loose dirt from the high-stress area around the mound to a depth of 3 inches.
  3. Place bricks. Place GameTime Clay Bricks as evenly and as close together as possible.
  4. Water and tamp. Water the cut area, then tamp the bricks into place.
  5. Fill. Fill the remaining half inch with the loose material you removed or with GameTime Mound Clay.
  6. Level and finish. Level and compact the new surface, then topdress with GameTime Pro infield conditioner.
